# 2.1.1 - the top binning tier is an ensemble again

One change, a correction: `--preset slow` runs three binners instead of one. The measurement that
had reduced it to one was against a wrong number.

## `--preset slow` = metaBAT2 + SemiBin2 + COMEBin

On the gold assembly the trio and COMEBin alone tie: 177 clean high-quality MAGs against 176. End
to end on per-sample assemblies the trio is ahead:

| arm | high-quality | clean | CPU-hours | GPU-hours |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| COMEBin alone | 61 | 57 | 80.9 | 1.7 |
| **metaBAT2 + SemiBin2 + COMEBin** | **64** | **60** | 120.7 | 2.4 |

A gold assembly favours a single strong binner: its contigs are long and its composition signal
clean, so a second algorithm has less to add. A real fragmented assembly leaves more, so binner
comparisons run only on gold assemblies understate ensembles.

**Why metaBAT2 is the third member.** The best trio by clean-MAG union is COMEBin + SemiBin2 +
MaxBin2, at 182 against this trio's 181. MaxBin2's one extra genome costs 24.5 CPU-hours against
metaBAT2's ~1.5, and 37.7% of its bins pass GUNC against metaBAT2's much cleaner set.

Complementarity per binner on the gold assembly: clean high-quality MAGs, and how many each finds
that no other does.

| binner | clean HQ MAGs | unique to it |
|---|---|---|
| COMEBin | 176 | 39 |
| SemiBin2 | 116 | 4 |
| VAMB | 80 | 0 |
| metaBAT2 | 35 | 0 |
| MaxBin2 | 33 | 1 |
| CONCOCT | 28 | 0 |

## Also in this release

Two scripts read a tier's cost back out of the run: `bench_work/preset_cost.py` and
`preset_gpu_hours.py`. GPU-hours are reported separately, since CPU-hours cannot price an occupied
GPU.

## Defaults that moved

`--preset slow` only. The shipped default (`--preset default`, metaBAT2 + SemiBin2) is unchanged.
